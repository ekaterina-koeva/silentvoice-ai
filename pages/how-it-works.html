How it works

Three steps, about a minute to set up, on a computer the person already owns.

The three steps

01

Calibrate

Three dots appear one at a time. The person looks at each one. The system measures how that particular person looks left, at the middle and to the right.

02

Choose

Cards of everyday phrases highlight one at a time. A held look to one side selects the highlighted card. A look to the other side moves on to the next one.

03

Be heard

The phrase is spoken aloud in a voice chosen on the device, and it appears in the session history so the conversation can carry on.

What it refuses to do

A tool that speaks for someone must not guess.

Calibration measures whether the signal can actually be told apart for that person, on that camera, in that light. When it cannot, gaze selection is not switched on and the screen says why. An honest refusal is safer than a feature that works most of the time.

When a reading falls between two directions, nothing is selected. When the person moves out of the position they were calibrated in, selection pauses and says so. Neither silence is a failure. Both are the correct answer to an uncertain signal.

Camera images stay in the browser. No video and no picture of a face is uploaded or stored at any time.

The engineering

A defect found by measuring, not by guessing.

Until 25 August 2026 the gaze reading averaged two measurements that pointed in opposite directions, one per eye. Because those directions are mirror images of each other, the average cancelled most of the horizontal signal. The thresholds tuned on top of it never had a chance.

The correction measures both eyes along one shared direction. Both methods were then computed from identical camera frames, so the comparison is between two formulas rather than between two sittings.

They are measurements from one developer on one machine. They say nothing about how the system performs for anyone else, and no such claim is made.
