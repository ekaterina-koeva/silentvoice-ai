Questions

Asked and answered plainly, including the awkward ones.

What equipment do I need?

A computer or laptop with a camera and a current browser. No eye tracking hardware and nothing to install.

Does it work on a phone?

It is built for a computer screen and has not been tested on a phone. There is no date for that.

What does it cost?

Nothing today. There is no payment system and nothing is for sale. Access during the assessment is by invitation and free.

Where does my data go?

The camera picture is processed inside the browser and never leaves the device. Nothing about a face is uploaded or stored. Generating a phrase with AI sends the selected words to an AI provider, and some speaking voices are produced on a Microsoft server, which sends the phrase text there. The interface names both of these where they happen.

Is it a medical device?

That is exactly what the regulatory assessment is deciding. Until it concludes, the honest answer is that the product is not registered, not certified and not clinically validated, and it says so on every screen.

Can it call for help?

Today the help button speaks a request out loud on the device. It does not notify anyone and it does not contact emergency services. An alert that reaches a chosen person is being designed, and it will never contact an emergency service either.

Why is the working prototype closed?

Because the product is being taken through a regulatory assessment, and whether a public deployment counts as placing a device on the market is one of the questions being answered. Closing it is the cautious answer while that is decided.

How do I get access?

Write using the contact form. Access is by invitation while the assessment is in progress, and it is given to advisers, professionals and invited testers.
